# PRD Quality Review — PRD: SawCunhaOS (Motor Fundacional) — Fase 1

## Overall verdict
This PRD's standout strength is verified brownfield rigor: every code-audit claim spot-checked against the actual repository (Company's missing enable/disable Use Cases, the reserved-but-unthrown `SCOS_COMPANY_004/005/006` codes, the single `UPDATE_LOGIN_STATUS` permission, the `vw_login_context` principal-profile-only join, Employee/Login's skeleton-only state) came back exactly accurate, and the ten `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at genuine tensions the audit surfaced, not safe checkpoints. Two structural risks temper that: the P0/P1/P2 build order silently assumes a P2-tagged feature (FR-19, Notification) is available to unblock a core P0-adjacent flow (FR-23, Login approval) with no risk or open-question callout anywhere, and the tenancy model (one ISP per deployment vs. shared multi-tenant platform) is architecturally load-bearing but never made explicit. Neither is fatal, but both should get an explicit answer before Architecture and Epics are cut from this document.

## Decision-readiness — strong
The PRD's discipline around surfacing tension is genuinely good, not decorative: `[NOTE FOR PM]` appears 10 times and lands on real, code-verified gaps (e.g. §4.1 FR-6, line 238, calling out that `BLOCK_LOGIN`/`UNBLOCK_LOGIN` don't exist as separate permissions today, inconsistent with Company's own precedent; §4.1 FR-7, line 244, naming a live bug where additional Profiles don't sum permissions in `vw_login_context`). `[ASSUMPTION]` is used 13 times, consistently with a stated reference value and a pointer to who resolves it (Architecture, security stakeholder, etc.), not as a way to dodge a decision. The Kill-Switch-vs-reactivation asymmetry (FR-13 automatic vs. FR-24 human-gated, line 269) is stated as a deliberate trade-off, not smoothed over.

### Findings
- **low** §15 "Nenhuma questão em aberto" overstates closure — §15 asserts every raised question has been resolved, but at least two load-bearing tensions never received the Open-Question/`[ASSUMPTION]`/`[NOTE FOR PM]` treatment the PRD applies everywhere else: the FR-19/FR-23 priority inversion (see Strategic coherence) and the tenancy model (see Scope honesty). *Fix:* reopen §15 with these two items rather than declaring full resolution.

## Substance over theater — strong
No findings. Four JTBD personas (§2.1), all load-bearing: Carla, Marina, and Roberto are named and reused as protagonists across 13 of 17 UJs, each UJ turning on a decision that traces to a specific FR — this isn't persona theater. NFRs (§5) carry actual thresholds or clearly-tagged placeholders (`p95 < 10ms`, `p95 < 300ms`, `< 1s`) instead of "must be scalable/secure" boilerplate; the Segurança/Confiabilidade/Observabilidade bullets point at concrete existing mechanisms (`%mask`/`%maskmdc`, `vw_login_context` staleness, `@Auditable`) rather than adjectives. The Vision (§1) names three specific liabilities (horas extras indevidas, contas órfãs, feriados municipais ignorados) sourced from the Brief's problem statement — it would not swap cleanly into another PRD.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is stated and followed: Zero Trust enforced at the API layer, P0/P1/P2 ordering that puts labor-liability and security ahead of geo/notification capability (§13.1), and named counter-metrics (SM-C1–C3) that stop SM-1/5/6 from being gamed. Where this breaks down is the one place the priority ladder and the FR dependency graph disagree.

### Findings
- **critical** P0-blocking dependency on a P2 feature, never surfaced (§4.1 FR-23, §4.5 FR-19, §13.1) — FR-23 requires that "o sistema notifica o aprovador resolvido... via modo síncrono do Motor de Notificação (FR-19)" before a Login can ever leave `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Every Login creation and reactivation in the system runs through FR-23/24/25/26 (§4.1's own framing: it "realiza a base de todas as Jornadas"). But FR-19 lives in §4.5 Motor de Notificação Híbrida, explicitly tagged **P2** — the lowest build priority in §13.1. Built in the stated P0→P1→P2 order, the team would finish Governança de Turno and Kill Switch (P0) with no Login able to complete approval and go `ACTIVE`, because the feature that notifies approvers hasn't been built yet. §9 Riscos e Mitigações calls out the analogous FR-4→§4.2 blocking dependency explicitly but says nothing about this one, and §4.1 itself carries no P-tag to reconcile against FR-19's P2. *Fix:* add this to §9 Riscos e Mitigações, and either pull a minimal synchronous-notify slice of FR-19 into the P0 wave or explicitly re-sequence FR-23–27 to tolerate Notification arriving later (e.g., a documented interim channel).

## Done-ness clarity — strong
Nearly every FR carries an explicit "Consequences (testable)" list with a checkable condition — status codes, error codes, timing bounds, state-machine transitions. Several FRs go further and mark exactly what's confirmed-implemented vs. spec-only (FR-3: "✅ Confirmado implementado e funcional"), which is unusually precise grounding for acceptance criteria.

### Findings
- **low** FR-9 consequence is adjective-only where a bound already exists elsewhere (line 332) — "Dentro do intervalo → requisição segue o fluxo normal, sem latência perceptível adicional" is exactly the "reasonable performance" pattern the rubric flags. The actual bound (`p95 < 10ms`) is stated three sections later in the feature's NFR block and again in §5, still `[ASSUMPTION]`-tagged. *Fix:* restate or cross-reference the bound directly in FR-9's consequence bullet instead of "perceptível."

## Scope honesty — adequate
§12 Não-Objetivos does real work — seven specific exclusions with the reasoning attached (e.g., Portal do Assinante, Radius/OLT provisioning), not template filler. §4.4's inline "Out of Scope (FR-18)" callout and the tag-automation deferral are handled the same honest way. The `[ASSUMPTION]`/`[NOTE FOR PM]` density (23 callouts across 28 FRs) is proportionate to a brownfield "Lançamento"-stakes document, not indecision — most are audit findings or reference values awaiting confirmation, not open indecision.

### Findings
- **high** Tenancy model never made explicit (§1, §3, FR-1) — §1 Visão frames the product at market scale ("núcleo de governança... para ISPs brasileiros de pequeno/médio porte"), but the domain model (§3 Glossário: Empresa = matriz/filial) and FR-1's uniqueness rules ("impede inativar a última matriz ativa **do sistema**" / "bloquear a única empresa ativa **do sistema**," `SCOS_COMPANY_005`/`006`) only make sense under a single-tenant-per-deployment model — a shared multi-tenant SaaS could never enforce "only one active company system-wide." That's a strong implicit signal, but it is never stated as a decision, `[ASSUMPTION]`, or Non-Goal anywhere in the document, and it is exactly the kind of load-bearing call (data isolation, auth boundary, deployment topology) Architecture needs handed to it explicitly rather than inferred from a side-effect of an unrelated FR. *Fix:* add an explicit assumption or Non-Goal stating the tenancy model, e.g. "[NON-GOAL: multi-tenant SaaS — one deployment per ISP customer; confirmed by FR-1's system-wide uniqueness rules]" (or its opposite, if incorrect).

## Downstream usability — strong
Glossary (§3) is comprehensive and its terms are used identically wherever they recur — Empresa/Filial/Departamento/Cargo/Funcionário/Login/Perfil/Recurso/Jornada de Trabalho show no drift across 17 UJs and 28 FRs. FR IDs (1–28, including the FR-23–28 block added in a later pass) and UJ IDs (1–17) are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates; every UJ is claimed by exactly one §4.x feature's "Realiza" line, and every FR referenced from §9/§14/§16 resolves to a real FR. No findings.

## Shape fit — adequate
Brownfield accuracy — the sub-criterion this dimension weighs most given the product type — is excellent and independently verified: the Company/Employee/Login state-of-implementation table in §4.1, the `SCOS_COMPANY_004/005/006` reserved-but-unused codes, the single `UPDATE_LOGIN_STATUS` permission, and the `vw_login_context` principal-profile-only join were all checked directly against the current codebase and match exactly. New-vs-existing is distinguished cleanly via the ✅/⚠️/❌ markers, and the backend-only scope (§0) is stated plainly. UJs are load-bearing given the multi-stakeholder cast (RH, técnico, gestor, Fase 2 consumer) — not overhead for this shape.

### Findings
- **medium** Approval-workflow subsystem is shape-mismatched to its container (§4.1, FR-23–28) — six FRs, a new primary Success Metric (SM-6), and the four-eyes/segregation-of-duty mechanism are folded entirely into "4.1 Fundação de Identidade e Organização," a section framed by its own description as CRUD/lifecycle ("cadastro e ciclo de vida de Empresa, Departamento..."). Unlike §4.2–4.5, §4.1 carries no P0/P1/P2 tag, so a reader skimming section headers has no way to see that a governance-critical, cross-cutting control comparable in weight to Kill Switch (§4.3, P0) lives inside it. *Fix:* promote the approval workflow to its own numbered feature with an explicit priority tag, or at minimum tag §4.1 and add a one-line signpost calling out the embedded subsystem.

Separately, the inline assumption at line 308 (RH must manually update the return date if a leave is extended, since "o sistema não tem como inferir sozinho") has no distinct §16 entry of its own — it appears folded into the adjacent FR-28 cancellation-timeframe entry, which covers a different assumption (the 5-day cancellation window).
- **Glossary drift:** none found. Terms are used with consistent case and form across the document.
- **ID continuity:** clean. FR-1–28, UJ-1–17, SM-1–6 + SM-C1–3 are all contiguous with no gaps or duplicates, including the FR-23–28 block added in a later revision pass.
- **Required sections:** all present for a Lançamento-stakes, brownfield, backend-only capability spec — Non-Goals, cross-cutting NFRs, Risks & Mitigations, Audit Trail, API Contracts/versioning, a dedicated Regulatory/Compliance section (§7, correctly called out as non-template), Success Metrics with counter-metrics, and an Assumptions Index.
